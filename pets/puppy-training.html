Puppy training with washable pads

Going outside is the goal. Until a puppy is vaccinated and old enough to hold it, or when you live high up or the weather is bad, a pad gives it one clear, dry place indoors.

56
What trainers say

A pad is a tool, not the end point

The American Kennel Club describes going outside as the ideal, but notes that training pads can play a role, especially with very young puppies. Its experts also warn that pads can be tricky, because the puppy learns two options. That is why the routine matters more than the pad.

Some owners keep a pad permanently, for small dogs in apartments for example. Others phase pads out within a few months.

5
Step by step

Six steps from pad to outdoors

One fixed spot

Put the pad in the same place every time, away from the bed and food bowl.

Go at the right moments

Take the puppy to the pad after waking, eating, drinking and playing.

Praise at once

Reward straight away. Do not scold accidents after the fact; it teaches nothing.

Move towards the door

Shift the pad a little closer to the door every few days.

Fewer pads

If you use several pads, remove them one by one.

Outside

Once the puppy is vaccinated and older, take it outside to the same spot instead of to the pad.
